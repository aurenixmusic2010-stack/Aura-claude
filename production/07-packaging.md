## Title options

All under 54 characters, per the brief.

| Title | Chars |

| What happens when America stops being hungry | 44 |
| The drug that broke America's snack aisle | 41 |
| The 50-year business model Ozempic broke | 40 |
| America built an economy on hunger | 34 |

**Recommendation: "Ozempic didn't kill Big Food. It repriced it."** It is the only
option that states the video's actual thesis rather than its topic, and the
negation in the first clause does the work a question mark usually has to. Every
other GLP-1 explainer in the lane is about the drug; this title announces
immediately that this one isn't.

"America built an economy on hunger" is the strongest fallback — it reads as a
thesis too, and it's short enough to survive mobile truncation entirely.

## Thumbnail

A supermarket aisle in flat cutout style, shrinking hard toward the vanishing
point, with a single injection pen standing upright where the shelves end.

**No face. No arrow. No open mouth.** The composition is the hook; adding a
reaction face would put it in the same visual bucket as the drug explainers this
video is deliberately not.

Palette: warm yellow shelves, navy ground, one coral accent on the pen. The pen
should be small — the aisle's scale collapse is the subject, and the pen is the
punchline.

## Positioning

Vox's own GLP-1 explainer cleared 3.5M views by explaining the drug. The gap this
video fills is that nobody in the explainer lane has told the **business** story.
Every packaging decision above should protect that distinction — the moment the
title, thumbnail or first fifteen seconds read as "here's how Ozempic works," the
video is competing with a well-served category instead of owning an empty one.

The cold open is built for this: it opens on food executives, not on a patient or
a pen.
