## ⚠️ Correction 1 — Medicare / Medicaid coverage (MATERIAL)

**Brief said:** *"Medicare and Medicaid still don't cover these drugs for weight
loss."* Sourced to "CMS policy, 2026."

**Status: FALSE as of July 1, 2026.**

CMS launched the **Medicare GLP-1 Bridge**, a demonstration running
**July 1, 2026 → December 31, 2027**. Eligible Medicare Part D enrollees access
GLP-1s at **$50 for a monthly supply**. The federal government pays **$245**, and
that same price is made available to state Medicaid programs. Federal law had
previously banned weight-loss drug coverage under Medicare, so this is a genuine
reversal, and it went live **27 days before this fact-check**.

**Impact:** this claim sat directly under Act Four's central argument. Act Four
was rewritten (blocks 68–72).

**Why the act got stronger, not weaker.** The original act argued *access is the
whole ballgame, and the gate is shut*. The corrected act argues *access is the
whole ballgame — and it's such a big deal that the federal government moved,
having banned it outright, and moved with an expiry date attached*. The
inequality point survives untouched because:

- $50/month is still a real recurring cost, and eligibility is limited.
- The demonstration **expires December 31, 2027** — it is a pilot, not a
  guarantee. That is a far better Act Four beat than a flat "no coverage."
- The Cornell income-split spending data is unaffected.

The through-line prop was adjusted to match: the paper barrier in Act Four now
**lifts partway and is revealed to be hinged**, rather than staying shut.

## Correction 3 — Cornell spending figures

**Brief said:** grocery spending down "5 to 8 percent"; restaurant spending down
"about 8 percent"; and an income split of **8.6% vs 4.2% above/below $125,000**.

**Status: the headline is right, the specific numbers aren't.**

The study is *The No-Hunger Games: How GLP-1 Medication Adoption Is Changing
Consumer Food Demand* — Hristakeva, Liaukonytė & Feler, **Journal of Marketing
Research**, published December 18, 2025. Numerator panel, ~150,000 US households.

Verified findings:

- **5.3%** decline in total grocery spending within six months of adoption.
- **8.2%** decline for higher-income households.
- **10.1%** decline in savory snacks — the single largest category drop.
- Spending at limited-service restaurants (fast food, coffee shops) also fell.
- Among households that keep using, lower spending persists at least a year,
  though the magnitude shrinks over time.

The **"4.2% below $125,000"** half of the split could not be verified in any
source and has been **cut**. The income contrast is now carried by the verified
pair — overall 5.3% vs higher-income 8.2% — which makes the same point honestly.
The savory-snacks 10.1% figure was **added**; it's a better visual anchor than
either, and it names the aisle.

Corrected in blocks 35–37 and 74.

Sources: [Journal of Marketing Research](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00222437251412834) ·
[Cornell / EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1110869) ·
[Food Business News](https://www.foodbusinessnews.net/articles/29532-glp-1-users-cut-food-spending-by-53)

## Correction 4 — Calories per capita

**Brief said:** *"2,160 a day in 1970 to near 2,670 today… five hundred more
calories."*

**Status: right shape, unverifiable endpoints.**

USDA ERS loss-adjusted food availability — the series that accounts for spoilage,
inedible portions and plate waste — gives **~2,050/day in 1970** rising to
**2,501/day in 2010, a 22% increase**. The raw (non-loss-adjusted) supply figure
is ~4,000/day, which is why unsourced versions of this stat vary so wildly.

Script now uses the loss-adjusted series and names its own limits: *"adjusted for
waste… about two thousand and fifty… by twenty ten, near two thousand five
hundred… twenty-two percent more food."*

This is the safest available framing. Availability is a proxy for intake, not a
measurement of it — worth remembering if anyone challenges the chart.

Corrected in blocks 19–20.

Source: [USDA ERS Food Availability (Per Capita) Data System](https://www.ers.usda.gov/data-products/food-availability-per-capita-data-system/food-availability-documentation)

## Correction 5 — "No regulatory standard" for GLP-1 friendly

**Brief said:** *"there is no regulatory standard in the United States for what
'GLP-1 friendly' actually means."*

**Status: true in substance, misleading as phrased.**

There is no federal definition of the term. But **USDA reviewed and approved the
labels** carrying the badge. As written, the line implies Conagra bypassed
regulators, which it did not — and that is exactly the kind of overreach that
draws a correction request.

Rewritten (block 51): *"U S D A reviewed the labels, but there is still no federal
definition of what G L P one friendly actually means."* Same point, survives
scrutiny.

Everything else about the Conagra claim checks out: **26** Healthy Choice items,
an **"On Track"** badge, **first major food brand** to put the phrase on a pack,
launched January 2025, and **no changes were made to the meals themselves** —
the badge flags products that already existed.

## Correction 6 — Discontinuation, and a nuance worth adding

**Brief said:** *"roughly half stop within a year"* — correct. Refined to the
precise figure, and one addition.

Danish national registry study, first **77,310** semaglutide-for-weight-loss users
without diabetes (median age 50, 71% women), presented at **EASD, Vienna,
September 2025**: **52% discontinued within one year.** Drop-off sharpest among
younger and lower-income patients, which researchers attribute to cost.

**Added:** an Endocrine Society finding (ENDO 2026) that **more than half of those
who stop restart within a year.** This independently supports the brief's
Cleveland Clinic point about real-world regain being milder than trial data
suggests, and it earns block 66 its "the direction holds" hedge.

Corrected/expanded in blocks 61–63 and 66.

Sources: [Medscape / EASD](https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/real-world-study-finds-over-50-stop-glp-1s-within-1-year-2025a1000obm) ·
[Endocrine Society, ENDO 2026](https://www.endocrine.org/news-and-advocacy/news-room/2026/sontha-press-release-endo-2026)
