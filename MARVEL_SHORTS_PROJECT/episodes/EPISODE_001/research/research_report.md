# Research report: The Metal Slowly Killing Wolverine

**Main question:** If Wolverine's skeleton is unbreakable, why is it also the thing hurting him?

**Main answer:** In the comics, adamantium is toxic to him, and his healing factor
is constantly repairing that damage. The two powers hold each other in check.
Marvel has written both sides of the experiment: take the metal away (X-Men #25,
1993) and his body later regresses toward a feral state; take the healing away
(X-Men #99, 2000) and the adamantium poisoning shows up within the issue. The
2017 film *Logan* (Fox, not MCU) shows the end state: a healing factor losing
ground.

## Counterarguments and uncertainties

- **Issue number for the poisoning beat.** Marvel's own article puts it in
  *X-Men* #99. One secondary source puts the depowering in #98, which is part
  of the same arc, and Looper cites *Uncanny X-Men* #380. The on-screen tag
  follows Marvel: `X-MEN #99 (2000)`.
- **Who caused the feral regression.** Secondary sources disagree about who
  attempted to re-bond the adamantium in *Wolverine* #100 (Genesis or the Dark
  Riders). The video does not name them, so this does not affect any line.
- **Logan (2017).** Some writers attribute his decline to age and wear rather
  than to poisoning alone. The narration only says his healing is losing
  ground, which every source agrees on.

## Scientific context

Adamantium is fictional. Real metal implants can cause local toxicity (for
example metallosis from cobalt-chromium joints), which is the real-world
intuition the story borrows. The video never presents adamantium chemistry as
real.

## Visual opportunities used

the two "tests" as an experiment framing, Magneto's pull drawn as the scanner
fault, and the tug-of-war as the answer.
